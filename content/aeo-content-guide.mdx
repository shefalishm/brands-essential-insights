## Begin with questions that influence decisions

Collect questions from sales calls, support tickets, proposals, on-site search and keyword research. Separate simple factual questions from comparison, evaluation and implementation questions. The latter often reveal stronger commercial intent.

## Build an answer-first outline

Use the main question as the page’s purpose. Provide a direct, qualified answer near the top. Then explain the reasoning, alternatives, process, risks and next steps under descriptive headings.

Write in clear language. Define specialist terms once. Use lists or tables when they make relationships easier to scan, not merely to decorate the page.

## Add information worth citing

Generic summaries are easy to reproduce. Contribute first-hand experience: a decision framework, original method, anonymised pattern, expert commentary, tested checklist or transparent data. Link claims to primary sources where external evidence is required.

Identify the author or responsible organisation. Keep commercial details, credentials and contact information consistent across the site.

## Reinforce meaning technically

Give each page a descriptive title and summary, clean URL, logical internal links and indexable HTML. Use relevant structured data only when it matches visible content. Ensure images have useful alternative text when they communicate meaning.

## Connect education to action

An AEO article can resolve a question without giving away the entire engagement. Explain what a capable reader can do independently, when specialist help becomes valuable and what a sensible next step looks like.

Measure citations and referral visits where tools expose them, but connect reporting to branded demand, qualified enquiries and assisted sales. Visibility matters most when it helps the right buyer make a better decision.
