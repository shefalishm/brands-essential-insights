## Mailchimp, Brevo or Kit: how should a small business choose?

Choose around the relationship you want to maintain. Mailchimp is worth evaluating for an audience and campaign-management brief. Brevo belongs on the shortlist when sending volume and wider messaging requirements matter. Kit deserves evaluation for a creator-led newsletter or digital-offer workflow. These are editorial starting points, not claims that the platforms serve only those uses.

The important question is what happens after someone gives you permission to contact them. They should understand what they will receive, find the messages useful and reach a relevant next step. A large template library or an AI writing button cannot compensate for a vague offer and an unclear subscription promise.

This guide uses official sources checked on **October 29, 2026**. It is research-led, not a completed hands-on trial. We have not sent test campaigns, measured inbox placement or compared conversion rates. Confirm the current regional offer, billing dimensions and features before purchase.

### Mailchimp: examine the audience and campaign scope

Mailchimp's official pricing describes email campaigns, templates, audience features, automation and reporting, with access varying by plan. Its pricing involves contact and sending limits, and relevant overage or promotional terms need review. Check the current checkout rather than assuming a headline offer describes the ongoing bill. See [Mailchimp's official marketing pricing](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/).

Our suggested evaluation begins with a sign-up form, a useful first message and a campaign to an appropriate segment. Ask which functions are essential and which are optional. A familiar product name is not a substitute for checking the tier that supports your actual journey.

### Brevo: examine volume and storage together

Brevo's official plan guidance describes email campaigns and a broader set of messaging and automation options. The selected email volume can determine contact storage allowances in relevant paid tiers; sending volume should not be treated as the only constraint. Additional channels and features have their own conditions. See [Brevo's official pricing-plan explanation](https://help.brevo.com/hc/en-us/articles/208589409-About-Brevo-s-pricing-plans).

Our suggested evaluation maps the list size, normal sending rhythm and any separate message types before selecting a tier. Do not assume email, SMS, WhatsApp and transactional requirements are all included in one simple price. Price only the channels the business can responsibly operate.

### Kit: examine the reader journey

Kit's pricing describes subscriber-based tiers and features including forms, landing pages, tagging, segmentation and digital products or subscriptions. Sequence and automation access differs across its offerings, and relevant commerce terms need confirmation. See [Kit's official pricing](https://kit.com/pricing).

We would evaluate it with a specific reader promise: subscribe for a useful topic, receive an appropriate introduction and reach a relevant offer. That is an editorial test scenario, not a claim that other vendors cannot support newsletters. Check the exact journey and any paid-feature dependency before making it central to your business.

## Define the subscription promise first

Write the sentence that explains why someone should subscribe. It should say what the reader receives and set an honest expectation about frequency. “Occasional practical guidance on choosing a business website” is a clearer starting point than a generic invitation to receive exciting updates.

Connect that promise to a real audience. A restaurant owner, a clinic administrator and a SaaS founder may need different examples and offers. You do not necessarily need separate systems for each, but you do need a reason for placing a person in a particular message journey.

Decide which messages belong outside the newsletter. A response to a contact request, an operational receipt and a promotional sequence have different purposes. Map those purposes before configuring automation, and check the applicable requirements for each with the appropriate responsible person.

Keep the initial promise sustainable. If the business can produce one strong monthly explanation, do not promise a weekly publication simply because the platform supports it. A manageable rhythm leaves room to verify facts, answer replies and improve the website pages linked from the message.

## Audit the audience before pricing the platform

Inventory existing contact sources and how each person entered the list. Record what information is available about permission and preferences. Do not assume a spreadsheet of email addresses is ready for marketing simply because it can be imported.

Separate active subscribers, people who have unsubscribed, undeliverable addresses and contacts held for another legitimate business purpose. Preserve the information needed to avoid contacting someone inappropriately. Review uncertain records with the person responsible for your applicable obligations before migration.

Identify duplicate and conflicting records carefully. A person may appear in more than one source with different preferences or details. Define which source is authoritative and what needs manual review. A bulk cleanup should not erase the evidence needed to respect a person's choices.

Use the resulting audience model for pricing. Count the records relevant to the vendor's billing definition, not just the rows in the newest spreadsheet. Ask how the proposed plan treats the record types and segments you need. Do not infer one vendor's counting method from another's.

## Model a realistic sending month

Describe the messages you expect to send: a welcome message, a recurring newsletter, a campaign to a suitable segment and any planned follow-up. Include the conditions that decide whether someone receives each message. This reveals overlaps that a simple list-size calculation misses.

Build a normal-month estimate and a busier campaign-month estimate. Use your actual intended audience sizes, while recognising those estimates may change. Translate the estimates into each vendor's current limits instead of assuming one subscriber tier implies unlimited sending.

Check the cost of the required users and features as well as sending. A plan may be sufficient for a basic campaign but not the automation, collaboration or reporting arrangement you intend to use. Record the required feature beside the plan entitlement so that the choice is reviewable.

Confirm billing period, currency, taxes, promotion expiry and overage behaviour at checkout. Keep a dated record for the purchasing decision. This guide deliberately does not quote a universal dollar price because regional displays, offers and configurations can differ.

## Design the first useful journey

Start with the simplest sequence that fulfils the subscription promise. A first message can introduce the subject, deliver the promised resource and explain what comes next. It does not need to present every service the company sells.

Give each message one main job. A guidance email can explain a decision and link to a deeper article; a campaign can invite a relevant conversation. Multiple unrelated calls to action make it harder to judge whether the message helped the reader.

Plan what happens when a subscriber is already a customer or has already completed the desired action. Do not let a generic sequence keep asking someone to do something they have done. Define those stopping or branching conditions before enabling automation.

Treat replies as part of the journey. Identify the person who will monitor them and where a useful conversation should be recorded. If the business needs a shared follow-up system, review the [CRM comparison](/insights/hubspot-vs-pipedrive-vs-zoho-crm) rather than leaving responses in an unattended mailbox.

## Write emails that deserve the click

Lead with a specific reader problem and provide a useful answer in the message itself. Do not make every email a teaser with no substance. Readers should be able to understand why the linked page is relevant before deciding to visit.

Use a subject line that accurately represents the content. Avoid invented urgency, unsupported savings or claims that the recipient's website has a problem you have not investigated. Clear language is particularly valuable when the business is still establishing trust.

Support commercial claims with appropriate evidence. An illustrative example should be labelled as such, and a testimonial should be genuine and authorised. AI assistance can produce plausible specifics, so verify every name, feature, number and attributed result before approval.

Make the destination continue the same conversation. If the email discusses a website planning checklist, link to the checklist or a relevant service explanation. Sending every visitor to a broad homepage adds work for the reader and makes the campaign harder to interpret.

## Use AI for drafts without outsourcing judgement

Give an assistant a defined task, audience and approved facts. Useful requests include proposing alternative introductions, identifying unclear wording or turning a long explanation into a shorter draft. Keep confidential customer information out unless its use has been appropriately reviewed.

Ask the human reviewer to inspect meaning, not just grammar. A concise rewrite can remove an important qualification or introduce an exaggerated claim. Compare the draft with the source material and make sure the tone reflects the business.

Maintain an editorial checklist for recurring campaigns. Include factual accuracy, permission to use images, working links, clear next steps and a responsible reply address. The checklist should be short enough to use every time, with additional review for unusual or sensitive claims.

Store the approved copy outside a single person's memory. Keep the source, version and intended audience together so another colleague can maintain the journey. AI can make drafting easier, but it should not make it harder to explain why a particular claim was sent.

## Test the complete subscription path

Use a controlled, authorised test address and follow the actual website sign-up path. Check the form wording, preference handling, confirmation behaviour and first message. Do not judge the system only from an editor preview.

Inspect the email on the devices and applications relevant to your audience. Check headings, images, text contrast, links and the ability to read the main point without images. A large decorative asset should not carry essential information that disappears when images are unavailable.

Test changes in preference and the unsubscribe path. Confirm that the relevant status is respected by the journey and any connected system. Check what happens when a test record re-enters through another form rather than assuming every integration preserves the same status.

Record the outcome and the plan tested. This is a proposed evaluation exercise, not a result we have obtained from these vendors. If an issue appears, establish whether it comes from content, configuration, integration or a product limitation before changing platforms.

## Prepare sending and operational checks

Before launch, follow the selected vendor's current guidance for sender setup and authentication. Assign responsibility for any required domain changes and confirm the intended domain carefully. Do not make DNS changes merely because an old tutorial suggests a value.

Keep marketing and operational requirements distinct in the plan. If the business also needs receipts, service messages or booking confirmations, identify how those are handled and who owns them. Do not move an essential operational message into a new campaign system without a controlled assessment.

Discuss access and data handling with the responsible team members. Identify who can export contacts, change automation, connect applications or send a campaign. Confirm that the plan's controls fit those responsibilities before storing the full audience.

Create a pause and correction procedure. Someone should know how to stop an incorrect sequence, identify affected recipients and correct a misleading page or offer. An automation is only manageable when the business can explain and interrupt its behaviour.

## Measure more than headline engagement

Choose the action that reflects the message's purpose. That might be a useful reply, a visit to a specific guide, a consultation request or an existing customer completing a task. Define the event before sending so the report has a clear interpretation.

Treat engagement figures as signals with limits. A dashboard metric is not automatically a qualified enquiry or a complete explanation of customer behaviour. Read the vendor's metric definitions, inspect relevant downstream actions and avoid comparing figures that measure different things.

Review the content and destination together. A clear email can still lead to an unclear service page, and a strong page may receive visits from an unsuitable audience. Look for the point where the experience stops helping the person make a decision.

Run small, understandable improvements. Change a specific part of the subject, message or destination and record the reasoning. Avoid claiming a universal performance lesson from a tiny or uneven sample. The aim is a useful learning process, not a dramatic percentage for a presentation.

## Migration and exit deserve a plan

List the forms, landing pages, automations, segments and integrations that depend on the current provider. Moving contacts alone does not recreate the system. Identify links and website embeds that will need review before choosing a transition date.

Check export formats and what information they include. Preserve the records needed to maintain preferences and suppression status, with appropriate access. Do not assume an export contains every historical event or that another provider will interpret each field in the same way.

Move a small authorised sample and inspect the result. Confirm field mapping, audience membership and automation entry conditions. Keep live sending controlled while testing so that migration does not trigger an unexpected campaign.

Agree when the old system stops and the new one starts. Account for scheduled messages and subscriptions that could otherwise overlap. Confirm retention and cancellation options directly with the relevant vendors rather than relying on a generic migration checklist.

## When a paid upgrade should wait

Delay an upgrade if the business has no clear subscription promise or useful content to send. A larger allowance will not solve that problem. Start with a coherent audience, a helpful message and a destination that explains the next step.

Do not buy additional channels simply because they are available in the same platform. Each channel needs permission, content, a response owner and an operating plan. Email, SMS and messaging applications should not become interchangeable destinations for the same unreviewed campaign.

Likewise, avoid complex branching journeys before the basic path works. A short sequence that respects the reader's context is easier to maintain than an elaborate structure nobody can explain. Add complexity when a real audience need justifies it.
