## Score the scope

Mark each item as simple, moderate or complex:

- Positioning and message development
- Audience or customer research
- Number of unique page templates
- Copywriting and original visual production
- CMS roles and publishing workflow
- Forms, booking, search or gated resources
- CRM, analytics and third-party integrations
- Content and URL migration
- Accessibility, languages and legal review
- SEO and answer-engine content requirements

Complexity compounds when several items depend on different stakeholders or systems.

## Build a dependency timeline

List discovery, information architecture, content, design, development, integration, migration, quality assurance and launch. For each stage, name the decision owner, required input and approval date.

Add time for content gathering and reviews. These are frequently more variable than implementation. Decide what is essential for launch and what can follow without weakening the core journey.

## Compare proposals fairly

Ask each potential partner what their price includes: research, copy, imagery, templates, responsive states, SEO migration, analytics, accessibility checks, training, warranty and ongoing support. A lower estimate may simply exclude work the internal team must absorb.

Use total ownership—not launch price alone—as the decision lens. A site that is hard to update or requires repeated workarounds can become the expensive option.
