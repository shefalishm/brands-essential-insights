## Start with the business problem

A dated visual style may need a refresh. A confusing offer may need new messaging and information architecture. A fragile platform may need a rebuild. These are different problems with different costs.

Before discussing colours or technology, document what the current site prevents: qualified enquiries, clear navigation, fast publishing, strong search visibility, or reliable measurement.

## Refresh, redesign, or rebuild?

### Refresh the interface

Keep the platform and page structure when they work, but improve typography, spacing, imagery, accessibility and calls to action. This is appropriate when the content journey is sound.

### Redesign the experience

Rework positioning, page hierarchy, templates and conversion paths when visitors struggle to understand the offer or find the right next step.

### Rebuild the system

Change the technical foundation when speed, security, integrations, editing or scalability cannot be fixed responsibly within the current setup.

## Protect what already performs

Inventory existing URLs, backlinks, organic landing pages and conversions. Decide which pages to retain, consolidate or retire. Every changed URL needs an intentional redirect to its closest replacement—not a blanket redirect to the homepage.

## Define success before design

Choose a small scorecard: qualified enquiries, consultation bookings, organic visibility for priority services, page speed and publishing time. A redesigned website succeeds when it improves the business system, not merely when stakeholders like the mock-up.

If the correct scope is still unclear, a discovery audit can separate design symptoms from messaging, SEO and platform constraints before money is committed.
