## MailerLite or Mailchimp: compare the same audience and sending job

For a small business, the useful MailerLite vs Mailchimp comparison is the cost of running your actual email programme. Count the audience according to each vendor's rules, describe the messages you intend to send and identify who will maintain the system. Then compare current quotes for an equivalent scope.

Our editorial recommendation is to choose the smallest complete workflow you can operate consistently. A large feature list does not help if the business has no useful message to send or nobody responsible for replying to enquiries. Equally, a free plan is not a good fit if it prevents a required customer journey from working.

**Research scope:** official sources were checked November 2, 2026. This is a research-led comparison, not a deliverability, interface or revenue benchmark. Vendor pages displayed regional pricing, so we have not converted those figures into purported US prices. Obtain a current quote for your audience, location and billing term.

## Current plan information: avoid copying an old free-tier comparison

[MailerLite's pricing page](https://www.mailerlite.com/pricing) currently lists Free, Comfort, Power and Enterprise. Free is described as up to 250 subscribers and 2,500 monthly emails. Comfort's allowance is tied to the subscriber tier; Power's unlimited sending is subject to fair use. Verify the required automation, form and seat allowances instead of assuming every feature is unrestricted.

[Mailchimp's marketing pricing page](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/) lists Free, Essentials, Standard and Premium, with a 250-contact free-plan limit shown during review. It also identifies contact and sending limits and paid-plan overages. We are not treating a promotional monthly figure as the ongoing price for every list size.

These are documented plan observations, not a complete quotation. Before buying, select the actual audience band and billing term, identify the required features and save the applicable terms. If an account uses legacy arrangements, confirm those inside that account rather than assuming a public new-customer page describes it.

## The important difference: a contact is not always a billable subscriber

[Mailchimp's contact documentation](https://mailchimp.com/help/about-your-contacts/) says subscribed, unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts count toward its monthly contact limit. Cleaned, pending and archived contacts do not. A non-subscribed contact has not opted in to marketing. Billing treatment is therefore different from permission to send a marketing message.

[MailerLite's billing guidance](https://www.mailerlite.com/help/plan-and-billing) describes a cumulative count of unique addresses active during the billing cycle. An address active earlier in the cycle still counts even if deleted later. Unsubscribed and bounced contacts are excluded subject to that cycle rule. Do not assume deleting records immediately reduces the current bill.

Our practical recommendation is to build an audience inventory before using either pricing calculator. Separate permission, status, source and billing treatment into different columns. This prevents the phrase “we have a mailing list” from hiding several different groups of people.

### Make an audience inventory

Record where each group came from, what they were told, when they joined and whether there is evidence of permission for the intended messages. Include existing subscribers, past enquiries, customers, suppressed addresses and records that nobody can explain. This is an operational review, not a legal determination about every jurisdiction.

Do not promote a customer or enquiry to a marketing subscriber simply because their address appears in a database. If permission is unclear, pause that part of the import and obtain appropriate guidance. A clean spreadsheet with unsupported permissions is still not a sound sending plan.

### Estimate sending from a plan, not an aspiration

Write down the broadcasts you intend to publish and the automatic messages a new subscriber may receive. Add separate rows for tests and any additional messages the vendor counts. Use the vendor's definition of a send. A campaign addressed to many recipients is not necessarily treated as a single unit of allowance.

Create an ordinary-month scenario and a busier-month scenario using assumptions you label explicitly. You do not need to predict explosive list growth. Ask what happens if signups exceed expectations, a campaign is delayed or an automation sends more messages than intended. The goal is to understand limits and decisions before they become urgent.

### Model an audience change without pretending it is a forecast

Use your current eligible audience as the base. Then choose a hypothetical higher band to see how the quote and allowances change. Label it as a budgeting sensitivity check, not expected growth. Record the action the owner would take before reaching a limit.

If the higher band needs a different subscription, compare the complete commitment. Avoid judging value solely on a free starting point that the business would immediately outgrow. Conversely, do not pay for a distant scenario when a smaller current setup is sufficient.

## Connect the website promise to the email programme

Before designing a signup form, complete this sentence: “Subscribe to receive…” with a clear description of the useful material the business can actually provide. A vague promise of updates gives the reader little basis for deciding and the editor little guidance on what to write.

A hypothetical local home-services business might offer a seasonal preparation checklist followed by occasional maintenance guidance. A small consultancy might send a practical explanation of recurring client questions. These are examples of possible editorial promises, not client campaigns or evidence of performance.

Place the form where that promise is relevant. The page should explain what the reader is requesting, which business will send it and how to obtain help. Keep a service enquiry separate from a newsletter request unless the reader deliberately chooses both. Check the actual implementation rather than assuming form placement establishes consent.

### Test the full signup journey

Use an authorised test address and follow the route from page to confirmation, audience record and intended message. Check whether the form's wording matches the subscription recorded. Inspect the success message on a phone and verify what happens if the address is already present.

Test an invalid entry and a failed connection. Someone should know how a failure is noticed and who investigates it. Record what information is collected and avoid asking for unnecessary detail. These are proposed acceptance checks for your build, not claims about a completed test of either vendor.

## Design one welcome sequence before adding complexity

Begin with a small sequence whose purpose you can explain. The first message should fulfil the signup promise. A following message can help the reader use the material or understand a related question. A commercial invitation should be relevant and clear about what happens next.

Write the messages before assembling a complicated branching diagram. This reveals whether the business has useful information for each step. If a branch exists only because the software makes it available, consider removing it until there is a specific need.

Give the sequence an owner. Someone must check claims, links, service availability and replies. Agree how a new subscriber exits or changes course when their circumstances change. Do not interpret the word “automation” as permission to leave messages unattended indefinitely.

### Keep a workflow specification

Document the trigger, audience, message, delay if used, exclusion and completion condition in plain language. Check that the chosen plan supports each required action. If it does not, simplify the journey or price the necessary alternative before publishing.

Include the failure path. If a form connection stops working or a promised resource becomes unavailable, who pauses the sequence and updates the page? A small explicit process is easier to maintain than a sophisticated system whose logic exists only in one person's memory.

## What to evaluate in the editor and reporting interface

Ask the intended writer to create a real draft using the approved brand material. Include a long heading, a useful image, a plain-text link and the contact information the business wants readers to use. Inspect the resulting message rather than an idealised vendor example.

Then ask the reviewer to identify factual claims, link destinations and the next action. The objective is an understandable approval process. If review happens outside the platform, describe how the final approved version is identified so an earlier draft is not sent accidentally.

For reporting, decide which question each metric should answer. Message activity, website visits, enquiries and sales are different observations. Do not present one as proof of another. Ask how the business will connect a specific message with an actual follow-up conversation where appropriate and with suitable data handling.

### Do not invent a deliverability winner

This guide does not contain a controlled comparison of inbox placement. We cannot rank MailerLite and Mailchimp on that basis from their pricing pages. A vendor's marketing statement is not independent evidence of your future sending results.

Instead, require the implementation team to check the current sender setup requirements, permission records and message review process. Keep the test limited and appropriate before a full launch. If a deliverability problem appears, investigate the evidence rather than switching tools based on a headline comparison.

## Migration: preserve permissions and continuity

A move should begin with a record audit and an explanation of what must be preserved. Identify active subscriptions, suppression records, forms, automated messages, important fields and external connections. Export capabilities and migration behaviour need confirmation for the actual accounts involved.

Map fields deliberately. A label in the old system may not mean the same thing in the new one. Decide how signup source, permission evidence and preferences will be represented. Keep a record of unresolved fields instead of importing them under a convenient but inaccurate label.

Do not move every record into the marketing audience as a shortcut. Maintain a clear distinction between people eligible for the intended campaign and addresses retained for another justified business purpose. Obtain appropriate privacy or legal advice where necessary; this article is not a substitute for it.

### Cut over the forms and sequences together

Plan how new signups will be handled during the move. Identify which form sends to which destination and when old automations will stop accepting new entrants. Avoid leaving two systems independently sending the same welcome sequence without a deliberate reason.

Run an authorised end-to-end test after the cutover and retain the evidence. Check both a new subscriber and an appropriate existing-record scenario. Keep rollback responsibilities clear, including what happens to records collected during a failed transition. Do not close the previous account until the business has checked the agreed migration scope.

## Choose the tool around a manageable programme

MailerLite deserves evaluation if its current subscriber and usage model fits the audience you intend to serve. Mailchimp deserves evaluation if its audience structure and required workflow fit your operation. These are starting points, not exclusive descriptions or a universal recommendation for one vendor.

For an existing account, first ask whether the problem is actually the tool. Weak messages, unclear signup promises or missing follow-up ownership can remain after a migration. Moving software may add work without resolving those issues.

For a new programme, favour a complete narrow workflow over several unfinished campaigns. A relevant signup page, a fulfilled promise, a useful follow-up and a responsible editor make a concrete first scope. Add complexity when you can identify the decision it supports.

### Make the final purchase decision reviewable

Store the dated quotes, audience assumptions, selected features and ownership responsibilities together. Record why the chosen setup fits the present programme and what would trigger a review. This helps the business distinguish a real need to upgrade from a feature that simply looks appealing.

Set a review after the programme has produced enough actual activity to discuss meaningfully. Examine the questions subscribers ask and the work required to keep messages useful. There is no need to pretend an arbitrary review date guarantees a commercial result.

## Give AI assistance a clear review boundary

If the team uses AI to help draft email material, begin with approved business facts and the actual audience promise. Ask for drafting assistance around a specific message, then have the responsible person review every claim, link and commercial invitation. Do not let a polished draft introduce a discount, customer result or service commitment that the business never approved.

Keep subscriber information out of general drafting prompts unless the business has deliberately reviewed the tool, purpose and data handling. Many useful writing tasks can begin with an anonymised brief describing the question and intended answer. Personalisation should serve a real reader need rather than become a reason to distribute more data across tools.

Approval should apply to the final message, not just an early draft. If automation can make later changes, define what it may change and when another review is required. A helpful boundary is to keep factual service claims and external sending under explicit business control. This is an editorial operating recommendation, not a claim that either vendor's AI features follow a particular approval system.

### Prepare for an absent editor

Document who can pause a scheduled message, correct a broken resource link and answer an unexpected customer reply. Keep a small inventory of active forms and sequences so a colleague can understand what is running. This is especially useful when an owner is away and a message needs a timely correction.

Choose one reviewable campaign as the first handover exercise. Ask the backup person to identify its audience, purpose and next action using the documentation. If those details are unclear, simplify the record before adding more automated work.
