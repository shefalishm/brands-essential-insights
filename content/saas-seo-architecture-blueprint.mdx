## The representative challenge

A growing software company has strong product knowledge spread across sales documents, help articles and demos. Its public website uses one feature-heavy page for several audiences, while the blog attracts broad informational traffic that rarely progresses.

## The architecture approach

Map queries and interview customers to distinguish four needs: understand the product, evaluate a use case, verify an integration, and compare alternatives. Assign each intent to the page type best equipped to satisfy it.

The proposed structure includes:

- Product pages explaining capability and outcome
- Use-case pages grounded in a specific role or workflow
- Integration pages with accurate setup and compatibility details
- Comparison pages with fair decision criteria
- Customer stories connected to the relevant product journey
- Guides answering implementation and evaluation questions

## Guard against duplication

Every page needs distinct purpose and substance. Repeating a template with swapped keywords creates a large site without a larger body of knowledge. Consolidate overlapping intent and use internal links to show relationships.

## A credible measurement plan

Track indexing quality, visibility by topic cluster, progression from educational to product pages, demo requests and qualified pipeline contribution. In a real engagement, publish only metrics whose definitions, period and approval are clear.

The lesson is architectural: search growth becomes more commercially useful when the site mirrors how buyers learn and decide.
