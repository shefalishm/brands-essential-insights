## Make the studio understandable beyond the portfolio

For an independent interior design studio, we recommend a website that helps a prospective client understand both the visual work and the service behind it. A visitor should learn what the studio offers, what role it plays and how to begin a project discussion.

Start with the actual commercial model. Does the studio provide consultation, a defined design package, project coordination or another service? Have the owner describe the boundaries. The site should not imply responsibility for work that the studio does not undertake.

This guide offers website and marketing recommendations. It does not provide building, construction or regulatory advice. Project examples are not invented here; the recommended portfolio process relies on work the business can verify.

## Clarify the scope before commissioning the site

Create a service sheet in language a new client can understand. Identify what each service includes, what needs discussion and where other professionals may be involved. Avoid using a broad “full service” phrase without explaining its actual meaning for your studio.

Separate services when the customer decision differs. A consultation may need a simple scheduling route. A larger project enquiry may require location, broad scope and preferred timing before fit can be assessed. Those journeys can share a visual identity while asking different questions.

Describe the stage at which the studio becomes involved. A visitor may already have a space, a concept or another project team. State the information that helps you decide whether to talk, rather than asking them to submit a complete specification before contact.

Use the [website development brief guide](/insights/website-development-brief) to organize the site scope. The brief should explain the business process as well as the desired appearance.

## Build portfolio pages from verified records

For each project, gather approved photographs, publication permissions, the studio's role and the details it can substantiate. Do not infer a budget, location, date or client outcome from an image folder name.

Explain whether the studio designed the whole space, contributed a defined part or supplied a particular service. Credit collaborators accurately where relevant and approved. A portfolio page should not make the studio appear responsible for another party's work.

Label concepts, renders and proposals clearly. A visual can demonstrate an idea without being a completed commission. The caption and surrounding text should preserve that distinction wherever the image appears.

Choose work according to relevance, not a target project count. A smaller selection with a clear explanation may be enough for the first release. Add further projects when the records and permissions are ready.

## Tell the project story without inventing an outcome

Use an approved sequence: the client's documented brief, the studio's role, the choices made and the result that can actually be shown. Where the client has not permitted detail, keep the description limited.

Do not add a fictional testimonial to complete a layout. Do not claim that a design increased value, improved wellbeing or reduced costs unless there is credible evidence for the exact statement and permission to publish it.

If a material or product is important to the story, verify its identity before naming it. A visual resemblance is not confirmation. Where that detail is unavailable, describe only what the record establishes.

Have the project owner review the page before publication. This is a recommended editorial step that helps preserve role boundaries and context. A fluent draft does not establish that the underlying facts are correct.

## Make images useful to readers and discovery systems

Google's [image SEO guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images) recommends descriptive filenames, surrounding context and useful alternative text. Apply that advice to the actual image, rather than stuffing a service keyword into every description.

Use alt text to describe relevant content accurately. Do not call a render a completed room or a stock image a client project. Keep captions and page text consistent with the image's provenance.

Ask the provider how images will be sized and delivered for the website. Preserve originals separately and use suitable files for page display. Test the portfolio on a phone as well as a large screen.

Avoid making essential project information visible only after a complex animation. A visitor should be able to read the studio's role and service detail even when the image experience is limited.

## Design an enquiry that opens a useful conversation

Ask what information the studio needs to assess fit. Location, project type, broad scope, preferred timing and contact details may be relevant starting points. The owner should decide what belongs in the first form.

If asking for a budget range, explain its purpose. Allow an appropriate route for someone who is still exploring scope. The field should support a conversation rather than silently promise the studio can work at any amount selected.

Distinguish the overall project budget from the studio's fees where that matters. Do not let a form imply that design, purchases and other professional work are included in one figure unless that is the actual approved offer.

Explain the next step after submission. Is the request reviewed, followed by a call or directed to a consultation? Use language that matches the process. Test what arrives in the studio's inbox and what the prospect sees.

## Present commercial information the studio can maintain

Publish fees only when the owner approves the scope and terms. For work requiring a proposal, explain which information is needed and how the discussion begins. Do not invent a market average or typical fee.

For the website project, request separate costs for copy, design, development, portfolio preparation, image rights or production, testing and training. Include recurring hosting and support in the comparison.

Define ownership of the domain, content and accounts. Ask whether the studio can add a project or change an enquiry field itself. A maintainable site should have a clear handover and a practical update process.

Reserve optional ideas for a later decision. A product store, extensive editorial library or interactive design tool should have a purpose and operational owner before it enters the scope.

## Build pages around meaningful services and places

Use distinct service pages when they explain different work. Avoid creating repeated pages with only a project type or city name changed. Each page should help a visitor make a decision the other pages do not address.

Represent the studio's location honestly. Google's [business-representation guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) emphasizes accurate real-world identity and location information. Review eligibility and the actual service model before setting up a local profile.

If the studio works remotely or accepts projects in several places, describe that relationship without implying offices in every area. Explain how the initial conversation and subsequent work are arranged according to the process the studio has approved.

Link a portfolio page to a relevant service explanation where it helps. The relationship should be clear: the project shows a verified role, and the service page explains how a prospect can discuss similar work.

## Write direct answers with the right qualifications

Useful questions include what the studio provides, when to enquire, what information is needed and how a proposal is developed. Answer directly, then give the conditions that matter. Avoid a vague slogan where a prospect needs operational detail.

Google's [AI search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) does not guarantee indexing or inclusion. A clear page can support understanding, but a provider cannot guarantee a number-one ranking or an AI recommendation.

Write a title that accurately identifies the page's subject. Use an accurate meta description too, while recognizing that Google may choose other page text for a snippet under its [snippet documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet).

## Use AI without changing the project record

Consider AI for organizing approved notes or proposing a page structure. A project owner should verify every detail before it becomes public. The source record, not the draft, establishes what happened.

Do not generate apparent evidence of client work. If a concept image is used, label it as such. Do not add fictional clients, budgets, awards or results to make an article sound complete.

## Plan ongoing marketing from the work you can show

Choose a service the studio wants to discuss and a portfolio page that supports the conversation. Verify that the content is approved before using it in social posts, email or a campaign.

Keep a record of questions prospects ask after viewing the site. Improve the explanation of scope, process or fit where confusion persists. Evaluate whether enquiries concern the work the studio wants and can undertake.
