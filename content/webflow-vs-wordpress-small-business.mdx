## Choose an operating model before choosing the editor

Webflow is a reasonable shortlist for a small business that wants a hosted site and a designed publishing system. Self-hosted WordPress deserves a look when the business needs a specific plugin, choice of hosting or an established editorial workflow and can assign ongoing maintenance. Compare both with the same pages and the same staff tasks.

Here WordPress means the self-hosted software from WordPress.org. Webflow's public pricing separates Site and Workspace plans. The WordPress project describes the available publishing, user and extension features. These are distinct commercial arrangements and each proposal should explain the actual implementation. [Webflow plans](https://webflow.com/pricing) and [WordPress features](https://wordpress.org/about/features/)

Official documentation was reviewed November 4, 2026. The tasks below are proposed evaluation exercises.

## Build one sample around the real job

Use a representative brief. Consider a hypothetical architecture practice with service pages, project records, a team page and an enquiry route. An office coordinator updates text and images. A designer occasionally builds a campaign page. The firm wants each project to show location, scope and relevant services in a consistent way.

Build the same sample project in both proposals. Give the coordinator approved content and ask them to create the page, upload an image, link a service and preview the result. Ask the designer to reuse the page structure in another context without modifying every individual project. Use this exercise to identify where each proposal needs custom development or training.

### Record the work before scoring the products

- **Structured content.** Ask how projects, services and people connect and who can change the fields.
- **Publishing.** Ask who writes, approves and publishes. Confirm the actual permissions in the proposed plan or installation.
- **Campaigns.** Ask what staff can build with reusable patterns and when the business needs a specialist.
- **Enquiries.** Trace a real test through the form, confirmation and staff mailbox or CRM.
- **Maintenance.** Identify who handles platform or plugin changes, third-party renewals and recovery.
- **Exit.** Ask for a sample export and an explanation of the systems you would need to rebuild.

### Recognize when a feature should decide the outcome

A firm might require a particular client portal or booking integration. Ask both bidders to demonstrate the required workflow on the proposed platform. Describe the specific job before accepting claims about flexibility. A generic integration logo may tell you little about data mapping, errors or staff responsibilities.

Confirm the plan, regional availability and supported usage. For WordPress, identify the extension, who maintains it and how it interacts with other requirements. For Webflow, ask which features the selected Site plan includes and which will rely on an external service. Keep the alternatives equivalent where possible and mark differences in the scope.

## Compare the true cost of running each website

For Webflow, price the appropriate Site plan, any required Workspace or seats, domain, external forms or scheduling and the hours needed for design or content changes. Webflow describes its Basic Site plan as a choice for sites without a CMS and lists CMS functionality with its Premium plan. Ask the bidder to price the current plan matching the actual content requirements. [Webflow Site and Workspace pricing](https://webflow.com/pricing)

For self-hosted WordPress, price hosting, domain, theme, required plugins, updates, backups and technical support. WordPress publishes update guidance that includes preparing backups. The maintenance agreement should identify what the hosting provider handles and who tests the site after a change. [WordPress update documentation](https://wordpress.org/documentation/article/updating-wordpress/)

Make a separate line for each recurring payment and record whether the business or the supplier owns it. Include the price at renewal and any usage assumptions. Track one-off design and migration work separately. Use the [small-business website costs guide](/insights/website-project-planner) to check which parts of the quote are still missing.

### Walk through a change request before signing

Ask both bidders to price an ordinary editing request and a structured change. An ordinary request might replace one project photo. A structured change might add a new field to every portfolio record and display it on project pages. Those requests use different skills. Agree which one the site owner can do and what a supplier would charge for the other.

Ask how the implementation will handle older records that lack the new field. A suitable layout might omit an empty detail. A migration could populate it only where the firm has verified information. Request the expected behaviour in advance so editors can keep publishing while the site evolves.

## Inspect SEO work at the URL level

Evaluate a representative service page and a record generated by the content system. Check the title, description, visible headings, internal links, canonical URL and rendered content. If pages are moving, ask for a redirect map and verify the old URL reaches the intended new one.

Define how staff will apply SEO settings to ordinary content. A consistent template can provide useful defaults, while editors may need a way to handle exceptions such as similar project names. Ask who reviews publication settings and what happens when a piece of content is archived.

Keep the testing target the same for both proposals. Ask for live mobile checks and a clear Search Console handover. If the business has an existing site with valuable links or enquiries, use the [SEO-safe migration checklist](/insights/seo-website-migration-checklist). After launch, use [the indexing troubleshooting guide](/insights/how-to-get-website-indexed-by-google) for individual URLs.

## Understand what an export gives you

Webflow's code-export documentation says a paid Workspace is required. It describes exclusions including hosted CMS functionality, form processing and site search. Ask what the vendor will rebuild if you plan to use the exported files outside Webflow. Treat a sample export as evidence for the exact pages and components you test. [Webflow code-export documentation](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961386739347-How-do-I-export-my-Webflow-site-code)

WordPress's built-in content export produces a file for supported content such as posts, pages and related records. A full hosting move also needs the other components required by the installation. Ask a migration provider to identify the database, files, uploads, extensions and configuration needed for your site. [WordPress Tools Export documentation](https://wordpress.org/documentation/article/tools-export-screen/)

Give portability an acceptance test. Request a representative content export and a list of media, accounts, forms and external services. Identify which pieces the business can keep using independently. Ask who owns the domain, where approved assets live and how a new developer would gain authorized access.

## Decide with the person who will run the website

Ask an actual editor to perform the ordinary update in each demonstration. Document the time and questions it takes for your own team. Then ask who would resolve a failed enquiry or correct a publishing error. Include the hours you expect staff to spend and the support they can rely on.

Compare each build against the role it needs to serve. A content-led company may value a repeatable publication process and a rich archive. An agency-led marketing site may value a defined design system and limited editor responsibilities. A business with an existing WordPress installation may want to compare a focused repair with a full migration before committing to either proposal.

Use the [website maintenance scope guide](/insights/small-business-website-maintenance-services) to price the operating work. If Squarespace is also under consideration, see the [Squarespace vs Webflow comparison](/insights/squarespace-vs-webflow-small-business).

## Decide who should be able to change the layout

Draw a line between changing approved content and changing the structure that holds it. A practice manager may need to update a phone number, replace an image and publish a project. A designer may need to change shared spacing, define a new component or adjust the template for every project. Give the roles different responsibilities even if one person currently does both jobs.

Run an ordinary editor exercise with a deliberate mistake. Ask the editor to add a long title, an unusually tall image and a project without a completed location field. Inspect the page on a phone. Check where the editor sees a warning, what can be recovered and how the business confirms the page looks right. This gives the builder useful feedback on both the editing instructions and the implementation.

Ask whether the resulting solution will use standard content features, a third-party tool or a custom arrangement. In a WordPress proposal, confirm what the builder is relying on from core, the chosen theme and any added plugins. In a Webflow proposal, ask which work belongs to the CMS configuration and which requires someone to edit the design. Assign an owner for both. ### Separate the systems needed to answer enquiries

Suppose the architecture practice wants an enquiry form that sends different requests to two teams. Build a test case for each route. Include a prospect with a small renovation request and a prospect with a larger commercial project. Ask the vendor to show the confirmation, who receives the notification and which record the business can review later.

Check the staff experience during an outage or an error. Someone may need to review an integration queue, check whether the form captured the record or restore a previous setting. Ask which team handles that and what evidence they need from the reporter. Give the provider access through the normal administration process and keep the business's ownership documented.

If a required integration has a separate subscription, record who owns it and which parts of the site depend on it. Include what the business would need to do if the supplier were replaced. A diagram of the page, form, destination and responsible team can explain the proposal without relying on a list of brand names.

## Compare an existing WordPress site with a proposed rebuild

If the business already runs WordPress, describe the problem before asking for a replacement quote. A slow editing process may come from unclear templates or permissions. An enquiry issue may come from a specific extension or routing setting. A better design may need new pages and content even if the underlying platform remains usable. Ask the supplier to identify the problem with evidence and price the work that addresses it.

Record where the existing site works well. Include pages that attract relevant visitors, useful content types, staff editing knowledge and approved integrations. Use that list in a migration brief. If rebuilding on Webflow, ask how each useful part will be implemented and checked. If rebuilding on WordPress, ask which parts will be preserved, retired or replaced.

### Make the transition an item in the quote

Put the current URLs, destination URLs, content assets and connections in one worksheet. Mark owners for each action. Where old and new pages use different fields, write down how the source information will be transferred and how omissions will appear. Run a trial using representative records before committing to the full move.

Plan how new content and enquiries will be handled during transition. For instance, a firm might finish copying portfolio records before release but still expect forms on the old site to receive enquiries up to the switch. Define the point when editors change systems, who owns incoming messages and how the business checks for missed records. Confirm what can be rolled back if a critical journey fails.

Ask both vendors to identify work that remains after publication. Redirect checks, account handover and review of early enquiries each take time. Include a named contact for questions during the handover and request instructions for the two or three tasks staff perform most often.

### Examine the build and care costs over the same period

Use the same comparison period and currency. List implementation work, platform and software charges, internal publishing effort and planned support. Keep unknown items visible and ask whether the contractor can resolve them through a short demonstration. For a website with ongoing campaigns, include expected design changes as a separate line item so the comparison reflects real use.

Avoid using an introductory plan price as the recurring cost. Write down when the initial terms end and which payment is due at renewal. Ask whether supplier-owned subscriptions can be transferred or whether the business must open its own account. Note the tasks that become easier or harder if the staff grow, change roles or hire a new supplier.

## Score the decision with the business owner

Create a compact scorecard with the following criteria:

- **Editorial fit.** An actual staff member can complete the agreed publishing task and understands where to ask for help.
- **Integration fit.** The important workflow succeeds and the team understands how to find an error.
- **Search implementation.** A representative public page exposes the intended content and metadata, with an agreed migration and indexing review.
- **Ownership.** The proposal identifies the people responsible for support, access, exports and renewals.
- **Cost.** Both bids cover the same period and explain the implementation, recurring costs and likely change work.

Give extra weight to requirements the business cannot compromise on. For a practice that depends on a specialized form, a weaker form workflow can outweigh an easier visual editing task. For a site publishing several project records each month, a repeatable editing flow can matter more than a rarely used export option. Set the weights before the demonstrations so the decision follows the original brief.

Finish by asking each supplier to show what a successful handover looks like. The business should be able to locate its accounts, edit a representative page, confirm an enquiry and find the support contact. If the proposal relies on a particular plan or extension, include it in the agreed scope and assign responsibility for keeping it current. This creates a clear record for the next person who runs the website.

## A final example for two different publishing teams

Consider a small firm where one coordinator publishes a project every month and a designer makes occasional campaign changes. Ask both bidders to show how the coordinator completes that job using the proposed permissions and written instructions. Give the coordinator a missing optional field and an approved photo with unusual dimensions. The exercise should reveal whether the template and help material support the real task.

Now consider a business with several authors, frequent updates and a need for a particular editorial integration. Ask the bidders to demonstrate a draft moving through review to publication and explain what happens when someone publishes an incorrect version. If one implementation requires additional software or a manual approval step, include that work in the comparison. Review how older content remains accessible when editorial staff change roles.

Ask the owner to sign off on four small deliverables from the demonstration. These can include a published sample, a completed test enquiry, a dated recurring cost sheet and a handover record with account owners. Collect an explanation of any feature that remains untested. If a requirement depends on an upcoming change or an uncertain integration, agree when the supplier will confirm it and what happens if the proposed setup cannot support it.

Use those deliverables when comparing the quotes. A difference in the build fee can reflect whether the supplier included migration, editing instruction or initial support. Read each line against the same requirements and ask the supplier to price anything missing. The comparison is complete when the business understands what it will own, what it will maintain and what help it can call on after launch.
