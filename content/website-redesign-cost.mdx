## Price the work behind the new design

A redesign quote should answer what will be changed, what must be carried over and how the finished website will be checked. A refresh of existing templates and a platform move can produce similarly polished screenshots while requiring different work. Ask suppliers to price the same pages, content and functional requirements. [Redesign or rebuild decision guide](/insights/website-redesign-or-rebuild)

Begin with a small inventory. Include the homepage, important services, location pages, useful articles and essential forms. Record their current URLs and who can approve their content. Group pages that share a template. Ask for both the number of templates the supplier will design and the number of pages it will actually populate.

## Compare three scopes before committing

**Targeted refresh.** Price revisions to the priority pages, navigation and contact path inside the current setup. Check whether the platform can support the new layouts and whether the existing content is usable.

**Redesign on the existing platform.** Price a wider template system, content revisions and implementation. Record which integrations and public URLs stay in service and how the vendor will test them after launch.

**Redesign with migration.** Include the new platform setup, content transfer, account ownership, redirects, forms and recovery steps. Give the business time to validate real content and representative customer journeys before release.

A bidder can recommend any of these scopes. Ask for the evidence behind the choice and the cost of tasks the business would need to perform itself. [Small-business website cost guide](/insights/website-project-planner)

## Ask where content and migration appear in the quote

Choose a sample service page and request a clear breakdown. One proposal might include interviewing the owner, writing the page, finding suitable images and entering the content. Another might include only the layout and expect approved copy on delivery day. Review both on the same basis before comparing their totals.

Ask the vendor to identify how old URLs will be handled. If the plan changes public addresses, agree destinations for pages customers still use and verify the redirects after publication. Include sitemap and internal link checks, along with a follow-up plan for important URLs. [Google's site move guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes)

### Price one representative integration

Give the bidder a real workflow to demonstrate. For a studio, a customer might open a service page, check a date and request an appointment. The proposal should say what the website controls, what the booking tool controls and who verifies the confirmation. If a subscription or integration must be replaced, include the setup and ongoing cost.

Add a plausible failure case. Try a date that is unavailable or a form submission with a required field missing. Agree how the experience should behave and where staff can find the resulting information. A handover should cover both the normal customer action and the exceptions the team handles regularly.

## Make the total comparable

Use one sheet with build, content, migration, integrations, launch checks and post-launch care on separate rows. Ask for the current platform and subscription charges on their own line. Add a row for work assigned to your team. Compare the initial payment and the first-year total over the same period.

Define what can change the quote. For example, a vendor might include two feedback rounds per template and price additional revisions separately. The business should know who can request a change, how the price is approved and whether the launch date will move. Write the expected review dates into the scope.

## Agree how the project ends

Ask for evidence from a representative phone layout, a test enquiry, the approved URL list and an account handover. Set an owner for each check. For phased work, define which pages or journeys are delivered at each stage and what triggers the next stage. This makes a smaller initial budget practical while giving the business a clear finish line.

## Work through a sample quote before signing

Consider a hypothetical home-services business with an established domain, several service pages and a contact form connected to a team inbox. The owner wants clearer enquiries and a more contemporary design. A focused proposal might revise the homepage, service template and contact path while preserving current addresses. A broader proposal might rebuild navigation and migrate the catalogue. Ask each supplier to identify the problem its scope solves and the evidence it used to choose that scope.

Make the content inventory concrete. For each page, record whether the business will retain, rewrite, merge or retire it. Identify where approved copy and photos live. If the agency will write a new page, ask how it will confirm the service details and whether a revision round is included. If your team will supply the material, set deadlines before work begins. Content availability can change the delivery schedule even when the visual work is straightforward.

### Compare the first year in a common worksheet

Create a column for each supplier and rows for discovery, templates, page population, content, integrations, migration, testing, training and support. Include all recurring charges under the same billing period. Ask whether a temporary service or overlapping subscriptions are required during the switch. Mark each row as included, quoted separately, business-owned or unresolved.

Keep the proposed price and any range assumptions visible. For example, a supplier might quote a fixed set of template revisions and leave the cleanup of old articles dependent on discovery. Ask how it will sample the old content and what would lead to a separate charge. The business should be able to compare the likely range of work before approving the design.

### Give launch checks a price and an owner

Ask the supplier to demonstrate a contact submission, a representative service page on a narrow phone and an important old link arriving at its correct destination. Check that the new website exposes the intended public pages and that internal links use the approved addresses. Ask how the team will handle incoming enquiries while switching form destinations. If a key workflow fails, identify who can decide whether to delay release and how customers will continue to contact the business.

Plan a short post-launch review. Confirm access, record the outcome of the agreed checks and list outstanding defects separately from new requests. Give staff instructions for the content changes they make most often. Include the responsibilities the business will need after the support period ends. The quote should make this handover observable rather than leaving it to a final meeting with no record.
