## Start with learner fit and a clear next step

For a small tutoring business, we recommend a website that explains who the service is for and makes the first conversation easy to arrange. A visitor should understand the subjects, levels and lesson formats before submitting an enquiry. The website brief should define what the tutor needs to know to decide whether the service fits.

This guide concerns website and marketing planning. It does not claim that a particular design will improve academic results or that tutoring guarantees a grade. Recommendations are intended to help you describe your actual teaching offer and create a manageable enquiry process.

Begin with an approved list of services and teaching arrangements. Identify what is available now and what is only being considered. Do not let a draft website present a planned course or future tutor as an existing service.

## Describe the subject and level precisely

Use subject names and levels the business can verify. If the offer relates to a particular curriculum, examination or qualification, have the tutor check the exact terminology and current scope before publication. Avoid vague wording that implies coverage beyond the team's expertise.

Explain the kind of support offered in ordinary language. A learner may be looking for help understanding a topic, preparing for an assessment or establishing a study routine. The page should describe the process the tutor actually uses, without promising the same result for every learner.

Create separate pages where the questions differ. A subject page can explain the level and teaching format. A general page can describe how the business assesses fit. If several pages would contain the same information, keep the structure simpler.

Do not create dozens of location pages for an online service that has no distinct local offer. Choose page subjects because they help a learner or parent make a decision, not because a keyword list contains many variations.

## Separate the lesson format from availability

State whether lessons are online, at a teaching location, at the learner's location or through another approved arrangement. Explain how someone can ask about availability. The format and the available slot are different pieces of information.

If the business reviews a learner's needs before accepting an appointment, label the action accordingly. A consultation request should not read like a guaranteed lesson booking. A confirmation should state what happens next and who responds.

For online lessons, describe only tools and capabilities the business currently uses and has checked. Do not infer recording, shared resources or platform features because they are common elsewhere. If a feature is important to a prospect's decision, verify it before naming it.

Keep a nominated person responsible for changes. If a tutor pauses a subject or a class becomes unavailable, the website and enquiry route should be reviewed. A maintained offer is more useful than a large set of pages nobody owns.

## Introduce tutors with verified credentials

Use accurate names, roles and qualifications approved by the people involved. Do not turn a degree, previous role or informal experience into a credential it does not establish. If a claim cannot be verified, omit it until documentation is available.

Describe the teaching approach using the tutor's approved explanation. Avoid attributing a method, award or specialization based solely on a photograph or profile headline. Let the team review biographies before publication.

Use genuine portraits where permission is clear. Generated imagery can support an editorial article if labeled appropriately, but it should not appear as a portrait of a real tutor or learner. The cover of this guide is illustrative.

If the business presents learner feedback, obtain appropriate permission and preserve context. Do not invent grades, quotes or improvement percentages. Where the business lacks publishable outcome evidence, explain the process instead.

## Make the first enquiry proportionate

Choose information because it helps the initial decision. A contact route, broad subject or level, lesson format and a short description of the need may be enough to start. Ask the tutor which details change how they respond.

Avoid requesting detailed learner records, personal histories or assessment files in a general marketing form by default. Decide how any later information will be requested and handled through the business's reviewed process.

If the service works with children, establish who should make contact and approve the wording and data process appropriately. This guide does not state a universal legal requirement. The business must assess its circumstances with suitable advice rather than adopt a generic web template as compliance assurance.

Test the handoff with a sample submission. Check what the tutor receives and whether the visitor understands the next step. An accessible alternative contact method can help someone whose situation does not fit the form.

## Explain fees without making unsupported promises

Publish only approved rates and terms. If lesson length, format or scope affects fees, explain the basis for discussion. If an introductory conversation has a charge, make that visible before someone submits a request.

Keep cancellation, rescheduling and payment information consistent with the business's actual arrangements. Have the owner review these statements. A website provider should not invent commercial policies to complete a template.

For the website budget, separate copy, design, development, booking integrations, content migration and training. Include recurring tools and maintenance. The [website project planner](/insights/website-project-planner) can help prepare the scope conversation.

Compare proposals through a real task: can a parent or adult learner identify the subject, understand the process and send a useful enquiry? Page count alone does not answer that question.

## Build the first site around maintained information

We recommend beginning with the subjects and levels, teaching formats, tutor information, the enquiry process and contact details. Add a small FAQ for genuine questions. The page structure should reflect the business's actual offer.

A custom learning portal may be a separate project. Define whether the requested work is a marketing website, a lesson-management system or both. These scopes involve different tasks and should not be combined implicitly in a quote.

Ask for a handover demonstration using your own content. Can staff change a subject, update a profile and adjust the enquiry link? Who controls the domain and platform account? What recurring subscriptions are required?

Defer a resource library if nobody can review and maintain it. A useful article needs a clear subject and an owner. Do not add pages simply to make the website appear larger.

## Make search content useful to a learner

Use accurate titles and headings that identify the subject and service. A page should answer who the support suits, what the tutor offers and how to discuss fit. Important information should be available in readable text.

Google's [helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) encourages content created for people and warns against producing to a supposed preferred word count. Our recommendation is to include the detail needed for the decision and remove repetition.

For AEO, answer questions directly while retaining conditions. “Do you teach this level?” should lead to actual coverage. “Can you guarantee an outcome?” should not become a marketing promise unsupported by evidence.

Google's [AI search documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) does not guarantee indexing or inclusion. Clear structure supports understanding, but rankings and AI recommendations cannot be promised by a website provider.

## Treat local discovery according to the real service model

Google's [business-representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) describe eligibility around physical customer contact and accurate business representation. Review the rules for your circumstances rather than assume every online tutoring business qualifies for a local profile.

If learners attend a real location, verify the address and arrival information. If the tutor travels, describe the actual area. If lessons are online, explain the format without implying physical branches.

Link useful related information. A subject page can lead to the enquiry process or a verified tutor profile. The links should help someone complete a decision rather than serve as a mechanical SEO exercise.

## Use AI as part of a reviewed writing process

Consider AI for organizing approved notes and drafting administrative explanations. Before using a tool, decide what information it can receive and who checks the output. Do not use it to invent experience, testimonials or academic results.

Review subject names, qualifications, lesson features and commercial terms against the business's approved information. A fluent draft is not evidence of accuracy. Remove unsupported claims rather than qualifying an invented fact into apparent credibility.

## Plan marketing from actual enquiries

Choose a subject or learner group the business can currently serve. Make the relevant page clear before promoting it. Keep the message consistent with the available teaching capacity.

Review what prospective learners still ask. Improve the pages that fail to explain format, level or next steps. Track suitable enquiries and the questions that prevent a useful conversation. Treat this as a proposed learning process, not evidence of a promised sales result.
